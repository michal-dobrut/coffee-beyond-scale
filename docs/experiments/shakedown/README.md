# Shakedown

Run the whole chain once, on real photographs, before the bench exists — to
find out what the bench has to be. It measures the instrument, not the coffee.

Every other campaign writes its protocol and its error budget before it has
data. This one could not: the questions it answers are the ones that decide
what those documents should say.

## What it establishes

That a photograph becomes a mass at all: session record, hand-placed corners,
rectification, paper-normalised segmentation, two pathways, one constant each.

And a ranked account of what the error is made of, which is the actual result:

- Half the measured area is the millimetre-wide band at a bean's edge, so where
  that band is cut matters more than anything else, and more resolution does
  not narrow it.
- Camera tilt is a bias of about five per cent, not scatter.
- An unbacked sheet is not a plane, and it outweighs the lens.
- The substrate is not the size its standard names it.
- Sampling resolution is not a limiting factor anywhere in the range tried.

## What it does not establish

Nothing about coffee. The constants are fitted on the frames they are scored
against, on one bag, at one roast, in one family of arrangements, and they
absorb every bias that multiplies every bean in the same direction. The
quoted in-sample error is a floor, not an accuracy.

## Sessions

- **`run-zero`** — 29 frames, four doses from a bare sheet to 50 g, hand-held
  under diffuse daylight. The bench findings above come from these.
- **`flash`** — two frames of 16 g under the phone's integrated flash, asking
  whether controlling the light buys anything. It does not: the count is
  untouched, but two frames of one arrangement disagree by ten per cent on
  area where the matched daylight pair agrees to within a rounding error.

## What it requires of what comes next

The sheet clamped to something rigid and flat; a matt, mid-grey, non-wooden
surface; the substrate's short edge measured with a caliper and written into
the session record; white balance locked before the first frame; a fixed
camera mount, or tilt used as a rejection filter. A bare-sheet frame in every
session, since it is the only one whose answer is known exactly.
